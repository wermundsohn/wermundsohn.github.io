Part A — Required PPMP Fields

Eight mandatory fields per RA 12009, Sec. 7 — each with specific rules and sub-steps

A1
Name of Project / Procurement
The official title used in all procurement documents, APP, PhilGEPS, and contracts
▾
📝 What It Is

The project or procurement name is the unique identifier that links the PPMP to the APP, the invitation to bid, the contract, and the PhilGEPS posting. It must be specific enough that it cannot be confused with any other procurement of the entity.

🔢 Sub-Steps to Complete This Field
1
Draft a descriptive working title that identifies the type of procurement (goods/infrastructure/consulting), the subject matter, and the intended location or scope (e.g., "Supply and Delivery of Office Furniture for the Main Building, FY 2025").
Avoid vague titles like "Office Supplies" or "Consultancy Services."
2
Check for duplicate names in the system against all existing PPMPs and prior-year APPs to avoid confusion.
System auto-checks for similar project names and flags potential duplicates before saving.
3
Assign a PPMP reference code following the agency's coding convention (e.g., PPMP-2025-001), which will carry over to all downstream documents.
4
Confirm alignment with the agency's mandate or program — the name should reflect the specific program, activity, or project (PAP) it supports under the GAA.
⚖️ Rules & Constraints
⚠️
The project name used in the PPMP must match exactly the name used in the APP, the ITB posted on PhilGEPS, and the resulting contract. Any discrepancy can be a ground for COA audit findings.
📜
Sec. 7, RA 12009: The APP shall at least include the name of the project/procurement. No government procurement shall be undertaken unless consistent with the approved APP.
Sec. 7 — PPMP / APP PPMP Form Field Auto-validated in system
A2
PMO / End-User Unit
Identifies who is accountable for the procurement and who will receive and use the goods/services
▾
📝 What It Is

This field identifies the Project Management Office (PMO) or the specific end-user unit / implementing unit that is initiating the procurement. It establishes accountability — this unit is responsible for the technical specifications, the statement of need, and post-delivery acceptance.

🔢 Sub-Steps
1
Identify the originating unit — the division, department, or project office that identified the need and prepared the Purchase Request.
2
Designate a unit focal person who will be the technical counterpart to the BAC and TWG throughout the procurement process. This person must be available for the pre-procurement and pre-bid conferences.
3
Confirm the unit is within the Procuring Entity's organizational structure. For decentralized agencies, identify whether HoPE functions are delegated.
In decentralized agencies, the head of the decentralized unit is the HoPE for that unit's procurements (Sec. 5(n)).
⚖️ Rules
📜
Sec. 5(j), RA 12009: The End-User or Implementing Unit is the organic office within the Procuring Entity that identifies, plans, prepares, designs, and implements the procurement project based on its mandate.
💡
The end-user unit is responsible for preparing all technical specifications (scope of work, ToR, plans). The BAC only facilitates the process — it does not write specs on behalf of end-users.
Sec. 5(j) & 5(n)PPMP Form Field
A3
General Description of the Project / Procurement
The technical narrative — what is being procured, why, how much, and to what standard
▾
📝 What It Is

The general description provides a concise but sufficiently detailed narrative of what is being procured, the quantity, the intended purpose, and the performance or quality standard required. It serves as the basis for the technical specifications in the full bidding documents.

🔢 Sub-Steps
1
State the object of procurement clearly — specify whether it is Goods, Infrastructure, or Consulting Services. These are treated differently under RA 12009.
Goods = supplies, materials, equipment, non-personal services; Infrastructure = construction/repair; Consulting = technical/professional services.
2
Specify quantity and unit of measure — e.g., "500 units of office chairs" or "1 lot rehabilitation of 3-storey building." Vague quantities will be flagged in the pre-procurement conference.
3
Describe performance or functional requirements — not brand names or models. Refer to relevant PNS, ISO, or other product standards as applicable (Sec. 11).
4
State the purpose / justification — link the procurement to a specific government program, activity, or project (PAP) and explain why it is essential for operations.
5
Indicate delivery location(s) — specify where goods are to be delivered or where the infrastructure project is located.
⚖️ Rules
⚠️
No brand-name reference is allowed in the description (Sec. 11). Exception: only for reasons of technical compatibility, interoperability, or preservation of supplier warranty — and the justification must be stated explicitly.
📜
Sec. 11, RA 12009: Specifications shall be based on relevant characteristics, performance or functionality requirements, or product/service standards by appropriate government entities or similar international bodies.
Sec. 11 — SpecificationsTechnical Specs DraftNo brand names
A4
Procurement Method to be Adopted
Which of the 11 RA 12009 modes applies — and whether procurement tasks will be outsourced to a procurement agent
▾
📝 What It Is

This field specifies the mode of procurement the end-user is recommending, based on the nature and value of the procurement. The BAC makes the final determination, but the end-user's recommendation in the PPMP starts the process. It also indicates whether procurement will be outsourced to a procurement agent (Sec. 15).

🔢 Sub-Steps
1
Check if the item is a Common-Use Supply (CSE) available in the PS-DBM catalogue (PhilGEPS electronic catalogue).
If YES → procurement must go through PS-DBM. No bidding required. Skip to Direct Purchase from PS-DBM. (Sec. 40)
2
Apply the GPPB Decision Tree (Sec. 6) — the system provides a built-in decision tree to guide the user through the correct mode based on: (a) type of procurement, (b) ABC value, (c) urgency, (d) number of available suppliers, (e) specialty/complexity.
3
Select the recommended mode from the 11 available under RA 12009 and document the justification. Competitive Bidding is the default; all alternative modes require specific legal grounds.
4
Indicate if procurement tasks will be outsourced to a Procurement Agent under Sec. 15 (i.e., the procuring entity lacks the capability). If YES, identify the government agency expert that will act as agent.

A5
Criteria for Bid Evaluation
LCRB (lowest price) vs. MEARB (quality-price balance) — a key strategic decision new to RA 12009
▾
📝 What It Is

This is one of the most significant new elements introduced by RA 12009. The end-user must recommend which award criterion should apply: the Lowest Calculated Responsive Bid (LCRB) — which awards purely on price — or the Most Economically Advantageous Responsive Bid (MEARB) — which weighs quality factors alongside price. This choice shapes the entire evaluation process.

🔢 Sub-Steps
1
Assess whether price alone is sufficient to identify the best value. For routine, commodity-type goods, LCRB is appropriate. For complex, innovative, or quality-sensitive procurements, MEARB may be better.
2
If recommending MEARB: draft the quality criteria and their relative weights. Under Sec. 61, price must be given a minimum weight of 15% and a maximum of 40%, meaning quality criteria collectively carry at least 60% of the total score.
Quality criteria may include: technical merit, aesthetics, functionality, approach & methodology, accessibility, tools & equipment, social/environmental/innovative characteristics, staff qualifications, delivery conditions, disposal measures.
3
Document the justification for the chosen criterion. The pre-procurement conference will validate this choice. The BAC, based on the end-user's recommendation, finalizes the criterion.
4
For Consulting Services: the criterion is always the Highest Rated Bid (HRB) using a quality-based evaluation — not pure LCRB. The financial proposal is negotiated with the top-ranked bidder.
⬡ Decision Gate — LCRB vs. MEARB
⬡
Decision Gate · A5
Is this procurement primarily quality-sensitive, complex, or innovative?
✓ YES — Use MEARB
Best value = quality + price
  • Define quality criteria in the PPMP/bidding docs
  • Set quality weight: 60–85% of total score
  • Set price weight: 15–40% of total score
  • GPPB may also authorize MARB (Most Advantageous Responsive Bid)
  • Must be disclosed in the ITB
✗ NO — Use LCRB
Best value = lowest compliant price
  • Standard for most goods and infrastructure
  • Bids ranked lowest to highest calculated price
  • No quality score computed
  • ABC is the absolute ceiling — no minimum floor
📜
Sec. 49 & 61, RA 12009: At the pre-procurement conference, the BAC shall finally determine whether the contract will be awarded to the LCRB or MEARB, based on the recommendation of the end-user unit.
Sec. 49 — Pre-Procurement Conf.Sec. 61 — MEARB (price wt: 15–40%)Sec. 62 — Consulting: HRB
A6
Time Schedule
The procurement timeline from PPMP preparation through delivery and acceptance
▾
📝 What It Is

The time schedule maps out all key milestones in the procurement — from PPMP submission to contract implementation to delivery. It must be realistic, account for all mandatory waiting periods under RA 12009, and align with the fiscal year budget cycle.

🔢 Sub-Steps
1
Identify the "need date" — the latest date by which the goods/services must be available to the end-user to avoid disruption to government operations.
2
Back-calculate the procurement schedule from the need date, building in all mandatory periods:
ITB publication: 7 calendar days · Pre-bid conference: ≥12 days before bid deadline · Bid evaluation: 7 calendar days · Post-qualification: 12 calendar days · Bid opening to award: ≤60 calendar days · Contract signing: 10 calendar days from NOA · NTP to delivery: per contract terms.
3
Consider early procurement activities (Sec. 12) — if the procurement is for the following fiscal year, the end-user may initiate the PPMP while the budget is still being deliberated, using an Indicative APP. No contract award until funds are approved.
4
For multi-year projects (Sec. 9): schedule must align with the Multi-Year Contractual Authority (MYCA) from DBM. The MYCA must be secured before procurement activities begin.
5
Build in contingency time for failed bidding (re-bid adds at least another full bidding cycle), protests (Sec. 83–86), and post-qualification failures.
⬡ Decision Gate — Schedule Feasibility
⬡
Decision Gate · A6
Can the need date be met given all mandatory RA 12009 timelines?
✓ YES
Proceed with standard schedule
  • Document milestones in PPMP
  • System auto-computes and validates schedule
  • Include buffer for failed bidding scenarios
✗ NO — Insufficient lead time
Consider alternatives
  • Invoke Early Procurement Activities (Sec. 12)
  • Assess if emergency/negotiated procurement grounds apply (Sec. 35(b))

A7
Source of Funds
The legal basis and account classification for the budget that will finance this procurement
▾
📝 What It Is

This field identifies where the money is coming from — whether it is the General Appropriations Act (GAA), a Special Purpose Fund, foreign-assisted (ODA/loan), internally generated revenue, corporate budget, or a local appropriations ordinance. The source of funds determines which rules apply and who approves the budget.

🔢 Sub-Steps
1
Identify the fund source category:
· NGA: General Appropriations Act (GAA) — specific Program, Activity, Project (PAP) and object code · GOCC/GFI: Corporate Operating Budget (approved by governing Board per E.O. 518) · SUC: RA 8292 (Higher Education Modernization Act) funds or internally generated income · LGU: Appropriations Ordinance of the Sanggunian · Foreign-assisted: ODA loan or grant agreement (treaty provisions may supplement RA 12009 rules)
2
Indicate the specific PAP / budget line item from the current year's appropriations and the allotment class (MOOE, CO, PS).
3
For foreign-assisted projects: confirm whether the treaty / loan agreement imposes procurement rules that differ from RA 12009. Under Sec. 4, treaty provisions prevail.

A8
Approved Budget for the Contract (ABC)
The ceiling for bid prices — the most financially critical field in the PPMP
▾
📝 What It Is

The ABC is the absolute upper limit on what the government will pay for the contract. It serves as the ceiling for bid prices — any bid that exceeds the ABC is automatically disqualified. It must be grounded in market research (Sec. 10) and lifecycle cost analysis (Sec. 13), and must be formally approved by the HoPE.

🔢 Sub-Steps
1
Base the ABC on the Market Scoping Report (Sec. 10) — price quotations, PS-DBM reference prices, PhilGEPS historical data, industry publications, or direct supplier consultations. The ABC must reflect the most advantageous prevailing market price.
2
Apply Lifecycle Cost Analysis (LCCA, Sec. 13) when relevant — especially for equipment or infrastructure. The ABC should factor in not just acquisition cost but operating, maintenance, and disposal costs over the asset's useful life.
3
For multi-year contracts (Sec. 9): the total project cost reflected in the MYCA or its equivalent document shall be the basis for the ABC. The annual slice of the ABC must be within the annual appropriation.
4
Submit the ABC for HoPE approval as part of the PPMP. The HoPE approval of the APP (which incorporates the PPMP) constitutes approval of the ABC for each procurement item.
5
The ABC is publicly disclosed in the ITB and PhilGEPS posting. It cannot be changed once the ITB is published without going through a formal APP amendment.
⬡ Decision Gate — ABC Validation
⬡
Decision Gate · A8
Is the ABC supported by documented market research and within the available budget?
✓ YES
ABC is valid
  • Attach market scoping report as supporting document
  • ABC approved as part of APP by HoPE
  • Budget Office issues CFA for this amount
  • Any bid above ABC is automatically disqualified (Sec. 60)
✗ NO
ABC must be revised
  • Re-conduct market scoping / price canvas
  • If ABC exceeds budget: reduce scope or request budget augmentation
  • If ABC is too low (all bids exceed ABC): revise ABC through APP amendment, re-bid
  • Padding or inflating the ABC = potential Sec. 92 violation
📜
Sec. 5(a) & Sec. 60, RA 12009: ABC is the budget for the contract duly approved by the HoPE. Bid prices that exceed the ABC shall be disqualified outright. There is no lower limit to the award amount.
⚠️
Single Bid Rule (Sec. 65): Even with only one bidder, the Procuring Entity must ensure the ABC reflects the most advantageous prevailing price. A sole bidder with an inflated ABC should trigger a review.
Sec. 5(a) — ABC DefinitionSec. 60 — ABC as CeilingSec. 10 — Market ScopingSec. 13 — LCCANo bid above ABC

Part B — Special Considerations

Six additional RA 12009-specific checks every PPMP must address

B1
Lifecycle & Environmental Impact Assessment
New mandatory consideration under RA 12009 — all PPMPs must account for total lifecycle cost and environmental footprint
▾
📝 What It Is & Sub-Steps
1
Conduct Lifecycle Assessment (LCA): Estimate the environmental impacts of the goods/infrastructure across its entire life — raw material extraction, manufacturing, transport, use phase, and end-of-life disposal. Required for all procurements under Sec. 13.
2
Conduct Lifecycle Cost Analysis (LCCA): Calculate the total cost of ownership — acquisition price + installation + operation + maintenance + disposal. For equipment, a cheaper unit with high running costs may cost more over its lifetime than a pricier but efficient one.
Lifecycle Cost = Acquisition + Operating + Maintenance + Disposal (Sec. 5(r))
3
Include disposal plan in the PPMP (Sec. 7): consider rehabilitation, donation, trade-in, auction, recycling, or direct negotiation for the asset at the end of its useful life. Disposal method affects the net lifecycle cost.
4
Ensure minimal negative environmental impact during the procurement lifecycle. Goods procured should be consistent with responsible consumption and production practices and national policy (Sec. 7).
📜
Sec. 13, RA 12009: Procuring Entities shall apply LCA and LCCA in project planning, eligibility and selection criteria, or contract implementation. Provided, That the GPPB shall issue appropriate guidelines on the application of LCCA.
Sec. 13 — LCA / LCCASec. 5(r) — Lifecycle Cost definitionSec. 7 — Disposal planning
B2
Green Procurement Criteria
Mandated by RA 12009 Article XII — environmental criteria must be integrated into specifications and bid evaluation
▾
📝 What It Is & Sub-Steps
1
Identify applicable green criteria for the specific type of goods/infrastructure. Examples: energy efficiency ratings, recyclable materials, low VOC content, ISO 14001 certification, carbon footprint labeling, water efficiency ratings.
2
Integrate green criteria into the technical specifications (Field A3) as minimum requirements or as scored criteria (if using MEARB). Green specifications shall be given greater weight in bid evaluation (Sec. 73).
3
Check if DTI-certified green products exist in the market for this procurement. DTI is mandated under Sec. 74 to develop a green product validation and certification program.
4
Note the green strategy being employed in the PPMP — e.g., specifying energy-efficient equipment, requiring use of recycled materials in construction, mandating low-emission transport, requiring ISO 14001-certified suppliers.
🌿
Sec. 72 & 73, RA 12009: All Procuring Entities shall establish a sustainable public procurement program. Green Public Procurement seeks goods and services with reduced environmental impact. Sustainability of products, materials, or structures with green specifications shall be given greater weight in bid evaluation.
Secs. 72–74 — Sustainable Public ProcurementSec. 3(g) — Sustainability principle
B3
Subcontracting Arrangements
If subcontracting is anticipated, RA 12009 imposes strict prior-approval and disclosure requirements
▾
📝 What It Is & Sub-Steps
1
Assess whether subcontracting will be necessary for the project (typically relevant for infrastructure and consulting services). Indicate this in the PPMP if anticipated.
2
Note the HoPE prior-approval requirement (Sec. 7): subcontracting requires the HoPE's explicit prior approval. The bidder must disclose subcontractors during the bidding process.
3
Clarify that the general contractor remains fully liable for subcontractor actions, defaults, delays, and negligence. The subcontractor is not a party to the government contract.
4
Include CPES applicability note (Sec. 7): the Constructors Performance Evaluation System (CPES) applies to the work of subcontractors as well, and subcontractors get credit for 100% of the subcontracted portion.
⚠️
Sec. 7, RA 12009: For locally-funded projects: (a) HoPE must give prior approval; (b) subcontractors must meet eligibility criteria; (c) bidder must disclose subcontractors during bidding; (d) general contractor remains liable for subcontractor's acts. The contractor and subcontractor share liability for safety and labor standard violations.
Sec. 7 — Subcontracting RulesHoPE prior approval required
B4
Design-and-Build Feasibility Assessment
For infrastructure projects — a new codified option under Sec. 14 that allows a single contractor for both design and construction
▾
📝 What It Is & Sub-Steps
1
Determine if the project is suitable for Design-and-Build (D&B, Sec. 14): D&B assigns both the design and construction to a single contractor, allowing quality assurance and effective cost/schedule management. It is best suited for projects where functional requirements are clear but detailed designs are not yet finalized.
2
If D&B is chosen: the detailed engineering requirement under Sec. 8 is waived — bidding may proceed without completed engineering designs. The contractor is responsible for both design and construction under a single contract.
Note: Standard infrastructure (Sec. 8) requires completed detailed engineering, surveys, and designs before bidding — D&B is the specific exception.
3
Consider architectural integrity: Sec. 8 also allows procuring entities to consider well-curated and aesthetic architectural design to ensure the infrastructure not only meets functional requirements but also contributes to its overall appeal and architectural integrity.

B5
Goods Must Be Brand New (Rule & Exceptions)
Default rule under Sec. 7 — with narrow, documented exceptions for specific sectors
▾
📝 The Rule & Sub-Steps
1
Default rule: all goods procured must be brand new (Sec. 7). This must be stated in the technical specifications. "Brand new" means the goods have never been used, are not refurbished, and are not reconditioned.
2
Assess if the exception applies: procurement of second-hand, refurbished, or reconditioned goods may be considered for: (a) military, defense, or law enforcement equipment or materiel — where it is most advantageous to the government, subject to DBM guidelines.
3
If invoking the exception: the PPMP must document the justification; DBM guidelines must be followed; and the criteria of durability, remaining useful life, sustainability, maintenance cost, and warranty must all be evaluated.

B6
No Brand-Name Reference in Specifications
A strict rule under Sec. 11 with narrow, documented exceptions — violations restrict competition and are a common COA finding
▾
📝 The Rule & Sub-Steps
1
Draft all specifications in terms of function, performance, or standards — never by brand name, trade name, model number, or catalogue number. Wrong: "HP LaserJet Pro." Correct: "Laser printer, minimum 30 ppm, duplex printing, network-ready, compatible with [specific network standard]."
2
Review specs against GPPB checklist — system flags any brand name, model number, or proprietary reference in the specifications field before allowing the PPMP to be submitted.
3
If brand-name reference is technically unavoidable: the limited exceptions under Sec. 11 are: (a) technical compatibility, (b) interoperability with existing systems, (c) servicing requirements, (d) maintenance, or (e) preservation of supplier warranty to keep the performance, functionality, and useful life of the equipment.
4
If invoking the exception: clearly state in the PPMP and bidding documents the reason or justification for specifying the brand/model. The ITB must disclose this. The default phrase is "or its equivalent."
⚠️
Writing specifications that are tailor-made for a specific brand or supplier — even without explicitly naming it — constitutes a violation of the competitiveness principle (Sec. 3(b)) and is a ground for administrative liability under Sec. 92(g) (acts with the intention of not complying with mandatory provisions).
📜
Sec. 11, RA 12009: Specifications for the procurement of goods shall be based on relevant characteristics, performance or functionality requirements, or product/service standards. Reference to a brand name shall not be allowed except for reasons of technical compatibility, interoperability, servicing, maintenance, or preservation of supplier warranty.
🖥️
System Validation: The digital procurement system scans the general description and specifications fields for known brand names, model numbers, and common trade names before allowing PPMP submission, flagging any hits for end-user review.

  • GOCCs / GFIs / SUCs: Governing Board or duly authorized official
  • LGUs: Local chief executive (Mayor, Governor)
  • Decentralized units: Head of the decentralized unit (subject to delegation limits)
What HoPE Reviews in the PPMP
  • Is the procurement necessary and within mandate?
  • Is the ABC realistic and grounded in market data?
  • Is the procurement mode appropriate?
  • Is the timeline feasible within the fiscal year?
  • Are green and sustainability criteria applied?
  • Is the procurement within the approved budget?
📜
Sec. 7, RA 12009: The APP shall be approved by the HoPE and must be consistent with its duly approved yearly budget or proposed budget in the case of the Indicative APP. The APP shall be formulated and revised only in accordance with the guidelines set forth in the IRR.
