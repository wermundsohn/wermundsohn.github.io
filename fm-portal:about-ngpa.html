Philippines: Government Procurement Policy Board issues Implementing Rules and Regulations of the New Government Procurement Act

March 10 2025

In brief

The Government Procurement Policy Board — Technical Support Office (GPPB- TSO) led by Department of Budget and Management (DBM) Secretary Amenah Pangandaman approved the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) of Republic Act No. 12009, or the New Government Procurement Act (NGPA), which took effect on 25 February 2025.

The NGPA, which was signed into law by the President of the Philippines on 20 July 2024, aims to promote efficiency by implementing an organized, uniform and straightforward government procurement process through the use of emerging technologies and innovative solutions (see our client alert on the NGPA here).

The NGPA IRR aims to address key areas in the NGPA for effective implementation, with the goal of creating a more streamlined, responsive and modernized procurement process.

In more detail

Some of the key provisions of the NGPA IRR are as follows:

Strategic procurement planning

The IRR requires Procuring Entities (PEs) to adopt a procurement strategy that includes fit-for-purpose and proportional approaches, such that conditions and parameters in the development of requirements, choice of procurement mode and implementation of contracts should be reasonably proportional to the needs of the PE and the nature of the project to be procured.

The IRR further provides that while the general rule is to procure brand new goods, the procurement of second-hand, refurbished, or reconditioned goods — such as military, defense or law enforcement equipment or materiel — may be considered to secure a contract that is most advantageous and offers value for money to the government. This is subject to guidelines to be issued by the DBM and compliance with relevant criteria, such as durability, remaining useful life, sustainability, maintenance cost and warranty.

Procurement by electronic means

To standardize and streamline the government procurement process and leverage the significant built-in efficiencies of the Philippine Government Electronic Procurement System (PhilGEPS), the system shall include the following features (items in bold are new features):

a. Electronic bulletin board

b. Electronic catalog

c. Virtual store

d. Electronic marketplace

e. Electronic bidding

f. Electronic small value procurement

g. Electronic reverse auction

h. Electronic payment

i. Registry of suppliers, manufacturers, distributors, contractors, consultants and service providers

Electronic marketplace

This feature allows PEs registered with the PhilGEPS to search, view, compare, select and purchase goods from qualified suppliers who meet legal, technical and financial requirements. It allows registered and qualified merchants to present their goods on the platform, whether as a result of a procurement activity, consignment or other appropriate contractual arrangements. which PEs may choose based on their needs and requirements and within their approved budget for the contract (ABC).

Electronic bidding

While the IRR of the Government Procurement Reform Act (GPRA) previously provided for the electronic submission of bids, the NGPA IRR now states that the electronic bidding facility will cover the entire procurement process. This includes procurement planning, posting of bid opportunities and bidding documents, pre-bid conferences, receipt and opening of bids, evaluation of bids, post-qualification, recommendation and award of contracts, contract management and implementation, and payment. These provisions apply to goods, infrastructure projects and consulting services. The GPPB is expected to issue further guidelines on electronic bidding facilities.

Electronic small value procurement

This allows PEs to resort to small value procurement electronically.

Electronic reverse auction

This refers to an auction feature where a PE registered with the PhilGEPS can issue an invitation to suppliers to submit bids on a specific date, time and duration for specific goods and services. These bids are based on defined specifications for design, quantity, quality, delivery, and related terms and conditions. Suppliers compete by outbidding each other with lower offers or amounts they are willing to accept.

Modes of procurement

The NGPA provides an expanded list of modes of procurement that PEs may adopt, consistent with its fit-for-purpose approach to procurement (items in bold are new features):

a. Competitive bidding

b. Limited source bidding

c. Competitive dialogue

d. Unsolicited offer with bid matching

e. Direct contracting

f. Direct acquisition

g. Repeat order

h. Small value procurement

i. Negotiated procurement

j. Direct sales

k. Direct procurement for science, technology and innovation

Competitive dialogue

This procurement mode employs a two-stage bidding process where the PE invites suppliers, manufacturers, distributors, contractors, consultants and service providers to a dialogue. During this dialogue, the latter propose solutions to address the PE's needs and requirements.

The PE may resort to a competitive dialogue if any of the following conditions are present:

a. The procurement includes innovative designs or solutions involving complex purchases such as, but not limited to, intellectual services, major information communications technology projects or sophisticated products. These may be goods or services, which in order to be produced or offered, would require a high level of technology, human capital, competencies or facility;

b. The contract requires prior negotiations with suppliers, manufacturers, distributors, contractors, consultants and service providers due to specific circumstances related to the nature, complexity, legal and financial issues, or risks associated with the procurement.

c. The PE cannot adequately establish or precisely define the technical specifications.

Unsolicited offer with bid matching

This is a mode of procurement where the PE may consider unsolicited offers from the original offeror on a negotiated basis for goods and consulting services.

The "original offeror" refers to the entity that initially submitted an unsolicited offer and whose proposal has been determined by the PE as necessary and involves a new concept or technology.

As determined by the Head of the PE (HoPE), the procurement involves a new concept or technology in any of the following instances:

a. It offers a substantial improvement over existing solutions, demonstrated through verifiable data, proof of concept or technical studies;

b. It incorporates features that promote emerging technology and sustainable solutions;

c. It involves state-of-the-art technology, as certified by a recognized independent agency with expertise in the field; or

d. It provides significant benefits to the PE in terms of efficiency, cost or outcomes.

The IRR provides that the PE should have invited comparative or competitive bids, which refer to offers submitted by the bidders through competitive bidding in response to a call for offers via an invitation to bid or a request for expression of interest.

Direct acquisition

This is a mode of procurement for common-use supplies and equipment (CSE) not available in the Procurement Service — Department of Budget and Management (PS-DBM), non-CSE and services with an ABC not exceeding PHP 200,000. In these cases, the PE may procure directly from any known and reputable sources without the need to conduct a canvass or request for quotations.

Small value procurement

This is a mode of procurement whereby the PE requests the submission of at least three price quotations for goods not available in the PS-DBM, infrastructure projects and consulting services.

The receipt of one quotation is sufficient to proceed with the evaluation of bidders, provided that the amount involved does not exceed PHP 2 million, subject to periodic review and adjustments of the threshold amount as deemed appropriate by the GPPB.

Direct sales is a mode of procurement for non-CSE where a PE directly purchases from a supplier that has satisfactorily delivered non-CSE to another government agency under a completed contract.

Direct sales may only be resorted to when all of the following conditions are met:

a. The procurement project is included in the approved annual procurement plan (APP) of the PE.

b. The original non-CSE was procured and awarded through competitive bidding, limited source bidding, competitive dialogue or an unsolicited offer with bid matching.

c. The government agency that originally procured the non-CSE has inspected and accepted it and has issued a certification that the supplier has satisfactorily performed its obligations under the contract.

d. The procurement for the same non-CSE by the PE must only be initiated once within six months from the acceptance of the non-CSE by the original government agency.

e. The contract to be executed between the PE and the supplier must be within the amount of the contract executed with the original government agency, and the supplier must offer the non-CSE to the PE at the same or lower unit price offered to the original government agency.

f. The supplier agreed to the terms offered by the PE and remains legally, technically and financially capable to undertake the contract with the PE.

Direct procurement for science, technology and innovation is a mode of procurement whereby the PE may procure the following goods and services directly from a legally, technically and financially capable manufacturer or supplier, or from a qualified startup business:

a. Supplies, materials, equipment and related services to be used actually, exclusively and directly in the conduct of research and development projects or activities

b. Goods which include products of a commissioned task by a PE that were processed, developed and manufactured to satisfy its needs and requirements, which include: (i) pre-commercial goods and services involving early market engagement to stimulate the development of new goods and services meeting specific government needs; and (ii) goods to be manufactured by qualified startup businesses, supporting the development and growth of new and startup businesses

c. Other goods analogous to the foregoing

Procurement of CSE

Under the IRR, all national government agencies, government-owned and controlled corporations, government financial institutions, state universities and colleges, and local government units may only procure their CSE from the PS-DBM, subject to its availability.

In procuring CSE, the PS-DBM may use the modes of procurement enumerated in the IRR or deal directly with manufacturers or exclusive distributors to gain access to the supply or obtain the rights to sell, distribute or otherwise deliver the CSE to PEs through consignment or any appropriate contractual arrangement, provided that the manufacturers or exclusive distributors comply with the requirements set by the PS-DBM.

Pre-procurement conference

Prior to the publication of the invitation to bid or request for expression of interest, the Bids and Awards Committee (BAC) is mandated to hold a pre-procurement conference for each procurement. However, holding a pre-procurement conference is optional for the procurement of goods costing up to PHP 5 million, procurement of Infrastructure Projects costing up to PHP 10 million and procurement of Consulting Services costing up to PHP 2 million.

During the pre-procurement conference, the BAC must decide whether to use the lowest calculated responsive bid (LCRB), the most economically advantageous responsive bid (MEARB), the most advantageous responsive bid (MARB) or the highest rated responsive bid (HRRB) as the criteria for contract award.

LCRB: used in the procurement of goods and infrastructure projects where the considerations for the award of the contract are the eligibility of the bidder, the responsiveness of the bid to the technical requirements and the lowest financial bid

MEARB: used in the procurement of goods or infrastructure projects where the considerations for the award of the contract are the eligibility of the bidder, the responsiveness of the bid to the technical requirements, and the determination of the most economically advantageous bid in reference to the quality-price ratio allocated to the technical and financial components of the bid

MARB: used in the procurement of goods or infrastructure projects where the considerations for the award of the contract are the eligibility of the bidder, the responsiveness of the bid to the technical requirements and the most advantageous bid in reference to the highest rated offer based on the quality component of the bid

HRRB: used in the procurement of consulting services where the considerations for the award of the contract are the eligibility and qualifications of the bidder, the responsiveness of the bid to the technical requirements, and the highest rated offer in reference to the weighted quality-cost criteria for the quality-cost based evaluation or the highest rated technical proposal for the quality-based evaluation

Eligibility requirements for the procurement of goods, infrastructure projects and consulting services

The IRR generally retained the qualification in the GPRA IRR that bidders for the procurement of goods, infrastructure projects and consulting services are subject to a 60% Filipino ownership requirement, with certain exceptions.

Sustainable public procurement

During planning and design, based on impact assessment, PEs are required to apply criteria such as energy efficiency, sustainable materials and waste reduction. Green specifications should be included in the technical and performance requirements for goods, infrastructure projects and consulting services. These specifications should focus on energy-efficient products, renewable or recyclable materials, and the reduction of greenhouse gas emissions, carbon emissions and pollutants in defining the type and objective of the project to be procured.

Domestic preference

PEs are mandated under the IRR to give priority and preference to Philippine products and services. This preference and priority must be guaranteed at all levels of the procurement process, including raw materials, ingredients, supplies or fixtures.

From 2024 to 2034, for Philippine products and services in sectors and economic activities covered by Republic Act No. 11981, or the Tatak Pinoy (Proudly Filipino) Act, this includes local goods, articles, materials, supplies, services and intangible products, which may be:

a. Unmanufactured goods, articles, materials or supplies wholly obtained or produced in the Philippines

b. Any goods, articles, materials and supplies produced, manufactured or assembled in the Philippines that meet the minimum local content requirements as determined by the TP Council

c. Services, which refer to the delivery of value or intangible acts through high-level skills, expertise, labor or knowledge-, technology- or process-intensive activities

d. Intangible products, which refer to properties without physical existence, including but not limited to computer software and products of the creative industry

The IRR mandates that the PE must award the contract to the domestic bidder. After 2034, the domestic preference will be subject to a margin of preference determined by the Tatak Pinoy Council, which cannot be lower than 15%.

The PE must award the contract to the domestic bidder if it secures certification from the appropriate agency that the articles forming part of its bid are substantially composed of articles, materials or supplies grown, produced or manufactured in the Philippines. Additionally, the domestic bidder's bid must not exceed the lowest foreign bid by more than 25%. The margin of preference is subject to periodic review and adjustment by the GPPB, as necessary.

Conflicts of interest

The IRR provides that all bidders found to have conflicting interests with each other will be disqualified from participating in the procurement process, without prejudice to the imposition of appropriate administrative, civil and criminal sanctions. A bidder may be considered to have conflicting interests with another bidder in any of the events described in paragraphs (a) through (c) below and a general conflict of interest in any of the circumstances set out in paragraphs (d) through (i) below.

a. A bidder has controlling shareholders or beneficial owners in common with another bidder.

b. A bidder receives or has received any direct or indirect subsidy from any other bidder.

c. A bidder has the same legally authorized representative as another bidder for the purposes of this bid.

d. A bidder has a relationship, directly or through third parties, that puts them in a position to access information about or influence the bid of another bidder or influence the decisions of the PE regarding the bidding process.

e. A bidder participated as a consultant in the preparation of the design or technical specifications of the goods and related services that are the subject of the bid.

f. A bidder lends or temporarily seconds its personnel to firms or organizations engaged in consulting services for preparing or implementing the project, in the event the personnel were involved in any capacity on the same project.

g. A consultant combines the functions of consulting with those of contracting or supplying equipment.

h. A consultant is associated with, affiliated with or owned by a contractor or a manufacturing firm with departments or design offices offering services as consultants, unless it includes relevant information on these relationships in the technical proposal cover letter and states that it will limit its role to that of a consultant and disqualify itself and its associates from working in any other capacity that may arise from the project, including bidding for any part of the future project.

i. If there is a conflict among consulting projects, the consultant, including its personnel and subcontractors, and any subsidiaries or entities controlled by it must not be recruited for the relevant project.

Future plans

According to the GPPB, certain provisions of the NGPA will still require the formulation of additional guidelines. These include guidelines for negotiated procurement for defense cooperation agreements and defense-related inventory items for law enforcement agencies, livestreaming procurement activities and the evaluation of contractor performance using standardized metrics.
