Phase 1 — Procurement Planning

The end-user identifies the need, prepares technical specifications, conducts market scoping, and submits for inclusion in the Annual Procurement Plan.

RA 12009 — Article II, Secs. 7–13
End-User / Implementing Unit
1Identify Need & Initiate Purchase Request▾
End-user unit identifies requirement and formally prepares a Purchase Request (PR) with initial technical specifications.
Required Documents
  • Purchase Request (PR) Form
  • Initial Technical Specifications
  • Statement of Need / Justification
  • Estimated Cost / Cost Estimate
System Actions
  • Create PR in digital system
  • Auto-assign PR number
  • Route to Budget Office for pre-check
  • Tag to relevant APP item (if existing)
Sec. 7 — Strategic Procurement Planning PR Form System: PR Module
🖥️
Digital System: End-user logs into the system, fills in the PR form with description, quantity, estimated cost, and urgency. System auto-validates against the approved APP and budget allocation.
End-User / Implementing Unit
2Market Scoping▾
Conduct market scoping to determine cost estimates, specifications, delivery lead times, and available procurement modalities.
Market Scoping Activities
  • Supplier/contractor consultations
  • Price sourcing from PhilGEPS catalogue
  • Review of industry journals & references
  • Analysis of previous similar procurements
  • Lifecycle cost estimation (LCA/LCCA)
Outputs
  • Market Scoping Report
  • Approved Budget for the Contract (ABC)
  • Recommended procurement modality
  • Technical specs draft
  • Delivery/completion schedule estimate
Sec. 10 — Market Scoping Sec. 13 — Lifecycle Cost Analysis Market Scoping Report PhilGEPS Catalogue
End-User / Implementing Unit
3Prepare Project Procurement Management Plan (PPMP)[ Expand process sub-steps > ]▾
End-user unit prepares the PPMP covering the cost estimate, specifications, procurement modality, schedule, and funding source.
PPMP Contents (Sec. 7)
  • Name of project/procurement
  • PMO/End-user unit
  • General description
  • Procurement method
  • Bid evaluation criteria
  • Time schedule
  • Source of funds
  • Approved Budget for Contract (ABC)
Special Considerations
  • Lifecycle & environmental impact
  • Green procurement criteria
  • Subcontracting arrangements
  • Design-and-Build feasibility
  • Goods must be brand new (Sec. 7)
  • No brand-name reference (Sec. 11)
Sec. 7 — APP & PPMP Sec. 11 — Specifications PPMP Form Timeline: Part of APP cycle
Procurement Office
4Consolidate Annual Procurement Plan (APP)▾
Procurement Office consolidates all PPMPs from end-user units into the Annual Procurement Plan; HoPE approves.
APP Requirements
  • All PPMPs consolidated
  • Within approved annual budget
  • Approved by HoPE
  • Posted on PhilGEPS
  • Consistent with yearly budget
Early Procurement
  • Allowed for next fiscal year
  • Based on Indicative APP
  • No award until funds approved
  • HoPE must approve Indicative APP
Sec. 7 — APP Sec. 12 — Early Procurement PhilGEPS: APP Publication
🖥️
Digital System: System auto-aggregates all approved PPMPs into the APP dashboard. HoPE reviews and approves digitally. Approved APP auto-published to PhilGEPS via API integration.

Phase 2 — Budget & Funding

Budget Office validates availability of funds, issues Certificate of Fund Availability (CFA), and coordinates with Accounting for obligation.

RA 12009 — Secs. 7, 9 | DBM / GAA Rules
Budget Office
5Review Purchase Request & Verify Budget Allocation▾
Budget Office reviews the PR against the approved APP and annual budget. Verifies if the item is included in the APP.
Budget Checks
  • PR must be in the APP
  • Available budget vs. ABC
  • Correct allotment class
  • Multi-year contractual authority (if applicable)
  • Fund source validation
Documents Issued
  • Certificate of Fund Availability (CFA) / Budget Utilization Request (BUR)
  • Allotment Release Order (ARO)
  • Obligational Authority (for multi-year: MYCA from DBM)
Sec. 7 — Procurement within budget Sec. 9 — Multi-Year Contracting (MYCA) CFA / BUR System: Budget Module
⬡ Decision Gate
Is the procurement in the APP and is budget available?
✓ YES — Proceed to Pre-Procurement ✗ NO — Return to End-User / Revise PPMP or wait for APP amendment
Accounting Office
6Pre-Obligation / Budget Reservation▾
Accounting Office earmarks / pre-obligates the budget to prevent double-spending. Records the obligation in the accounting system.
Actions
  • Record budget reservation/pre-obligation
  • Assign Obligation Request (ObR) number
  • Link ObR to PR and ABC
  • Coordinate with Budget on fund release
Documents
  • Obligation Request and Status (ORS)
  • Registry of Allotments and Obligations
  • Disbursement Voucher (pre-filled header)
ORS / ObR System: Accounting Module — IFMIS integration Sec. 21 — Electronic Payment System (IFMIS)

Phase 3 — Bidding Documents & Pre-Procurement

BAC determines the procurement mode, prepares bidding documents, holds a pre-procurement conference, and ensures readiness for public bidding.

RA 12009 — Articles V, VI, VII | Secs. 26, 41–42, 47–49
Procurement Office / BAC
7Determine Mode of Procurement▾
BAC reviews the PPMP/APP and determines the appropriate procurement mode based on the nature, amount, and urgency of the procurement.
RA 12009 Procurement Modes (Sec. 26)
  • Competitive Bidding — default mode
  • Limited Source Bidding — specialized goods
  • Competitive Dialogue — complex/innovative (NEW)
  • Direct Contracting — proprietary
  • Small Value Procurement — ≤ ₱2M
  • Negotiated Procurement — specific grounds
  • Direct Procurement for STI — startups (NEW)
Decision Factors
  • ABC / contract value
  • Nature and complexity of goods/service
  • Urgency / emergency
  • Availability in PS-DBM catalogue
  • Number of eligible suppliers
  • GPPB Decision Tree (Sec. 6)
Sec. 26 — Modes of Procurement Sec. 6 — GPPB Decision Tree Sec. 40 — CSE from PS-DBM
🖥️
Digital System: Built-in GPPB-compliant decision tree guides BAC/Procurement Officer in selecting the correct mode based on inputs. System auto-checks if item is available in PS-DBM/PhilGEPS catalogue first.
BAC + End-User Unit
8Prepare Bidding Documents▾
BAC (with the end-user unit) prepares the complete bidding documents following GPPB standard forms.
Bidding Document Contents (Sec. 47)
  • Approved Budget for the Contract (ABC)
  • Invitation to Bid (ITB)
  • Instructions to Bidders (eligibility, evaluation criteria, LCRB or MEARB)
  • Terms of Reference / Scope of Work
  • Eligibility Requirements
  • Plans & Technical Specifications
  • Price Form & Bill of Quantities
  • Delivery / Completion Schedule
  • Bid Security form & amount
  • Performance Security form & amount
  • Form of Contract & Special/General Conditions
Key Requirements
  • Use GPPB standard forms only
  • No brand-name references (Sec. 11)
  • Specify: LCRB or MEARB award criteria
  • If MEARB: define quality-price ratio (min 15%, max 40% for price)
  • Include green procurement criteria (Sec. 73)
  • Disclose relations affidavit requirement (Sec. 81)
  • Include beneficial ownership declaration requirement (Sec. 82)
Sec. 47 — Bidding Documents Sec. 48 — Access to Information Sec. 61 — MEARB Criteria GPPB Standard Forms
BAC
9Pre-Procurement Conference▾
BAC holds a mandatory pre-procurement conference to validate readiness, confirm fund availability, and review bidding documents before publication.
Conference Agenda (Sec. 49)
  • Confirm fund certification (CFA)
  • Confirm technical specs completeness
  • Determine LCRB vs. MEARB award criterion
  • Review draft ITB and bidding documents
  • Set bidding schedule and milestones
  • Confirm observer invitations
Attendees
  • BAC members
  • End-user unit / implementing unit
  • BAC Secretariat
  • Consultants (if any)
  • COA representative (optional)
Sec. 49 — Pre-Procurement Conference Sec. 38 — Video Recording (mandatory for large procurements) Minutes of Pre-Procurement Conference
🎥
Mandatory Video Recording (Sec. 38): For Goods > ₱10M, Infrastructure > ₱20M, Consulting > ₱5M — system must initiate and manage video recording. Stored for 5 years; accessible to public upon request.
🏷️

Phase 4 — Invitation to Bid & Bidding Process

Publication of the Invitation to Bid, pre-bid conference, eligibility screening, bid submission, and public bid opening.

RA 12009 — Articles VII–VIII | Secs. 50–58
BAC / Procurement Office
10Publish Invitation to Bid (ITB)▾
Post ITB continuously for 7 calendar days across all required channels simultaneously.
Publication Channels (Sec. 50)
  • PhilGEPS — primary & mandatory
  • Agency website
  • Agency social media platforms (new)
  • Conspicuous place in agency premises
  • Newspaper of national circulation (fallback only)
ITB Must Contain (Sec. 50)
  • Brief description of procurement
  • Eligibility criteria and evaluation method
  • LCRB or MEARB award basis
  • Deadlines for eligibility, pre-bid, bid opening
  • ABC and source of funds
  • Bidding document availability period & cost
  • Contract duration
Sec. 50 — Publication (7 calendar days) PhilGEPS Auto-Post via API Duration: 7 consecutive calendar days
BAC
11Pre-Bid Conference▾
BAC holds at least one pre-bid conference at least 12 calendar days before the bid deadline. Prospective bidders may request additional conferences.
Conference Conduct (Sec. 51)
  • Clarify bidding documents
  • BAC may assist bidders in document prep (new under RA 12009)
  • Issue Supplemental Bid Bulletins if needed
  • All clarifications made in writing
  • COA representative invited
  • At least 2 external observers invited
Observers Required (Sec. 43)
  • 1 from relevant private sector group
  • 1 from CSO or people's organization
  • COA representative
  • Must have no conflict of interest
  • Must submit separate observer's report to HoPE, PhilGEPS, and COA
Sec. 51 — Pre-Bid Conference (≥12 days before deadline) Sec. 43 — Observers Minutes of Pre-Bid Conference Held: ≥12 calendar days before bid deadline
Suppliers / Bidders
12Eligibility Screening▾
Prospective bidders submit eligibility documents. BAC screens based on the valid and updated PhilGEPS Certificate of Registration and Membership.
Eligibility Documents (Sec. 52)
  • Valid PhilGEPS Certificate of Registration & Membership (primary document)
  • Legal Eligibility (permits, licenses, registrations)
  • Technical Eligibility (experience, track record)
  • Financial Eligibility (capacity)
  • Sworn Disclosure of Relations (Sec. 81)
  • Beneficial Ownership Declaration (Sec. 82)
System Checks
  • Cross-check PhilGEPS registration status
  • Verify against GPPB blacklist/suspension list
  • Verify BIR tax clearance (via interconnected database, Sec. 23)
  • CIAP/PCAB license check (for infrastructure)
  • Beneficial ownership registry check
Sec. 52 — Eligibility Requirements Sec. 81 — Disclosure of Relations Sec. 82 — Beneficial Ownership Sec. 23 — Systems Interconnectivity PhilGEPS Integration
Suppliers / Bidders
13Bid Submission▾
Eligible bidders submit bids in two separate sealed envelopes (or electronic equivalent): Technical Component and Financial Component, with Bid Security.
Envelope 1 — Technical (Sec. 54)
  • Technical specifications compliance
  • Organizational chart & personnel
  • Track record / experience documents
  • Equipment list (if applicable)
  • Project methodology / work plan
  • Sworn Disclosure of Relations
  • Beneficial Ownership form
Envelope 2 — Financial (Sec. 54)
  • Bid Form with unit and total prices
  • Bill of Quantities (infrastructure)
  • Bid Security (callable-on-demand)
  • Price must be ≤ ABC (ceiling)
  • No minimum price floor
Sec. 54 — Bid Submission Sec. 56 — Bid Security (callable-on-demand) Optional: Electronic submission via PhilGEPS Deadline: Fixed by BAC in ITB
BAC
14Bid Opening (Public)▾
BAC publicly opens all bids no later than 1 calendar day after the bid submission deadline. Livestreamed to the public.
Bid Opening Process (Sec. 58)
  • Held: not later than 1 day after deadline
  • All bids opened publicly
  • Technical envelopes opened first
  • Financial envelopes opened only for technically compliant bids
  • Bid prices recorded in Abstract of Bids
  • Minutes prepared and made public
Transparency Requirements
  • COA representative present
  • External observers present
  • Video recording — mandatory for large procurements (Sec. 38)
  • Livestreamed on agency website/social media (Sec. 38)
  • Open to any prospective bidder/public
Sec. 58 — Bid Opening Sec. 38 — Video Recording & Livestreaming Abstract of Bids; Minutes of Bid Opening Held: ≤1 calendar day after submission deadline
⚖️

Phase 5 — Bid Evaluation & Post-Qualification

BAC evaluates bids (technical pass/fail, then financial ranking or quality-price scoring), conducts post-qualification of the winning bidder, and determines the LCRB, MEARB, or HRRB.

RA 12009 — Articles IX–X | Secs. 59–65
BAC / TWG
15Preliminary Examination of Bids (Pass/Fail)▾
BAC checks if all required documents in the technical component are present. Bids with complete documents proceed to financial evaluation.
Pass/Fail Criteria (Sec. 59)
  • Completeness of required documents
  • Proper signatures/notarization
  • Bid Security submitted and valid
  • Joint Venture agreement (if applicable)
  • Disclosure affidavits present
  • Technical compliance with specifications
Timeline
  • Full evaluation process (Sec. 61): within 7 calendar days from deadline for receipt of proposals
  • TWG may be created to assist BAC in technical evaluation
Sec. 59 — Preliminary Examination Full evaluation: within 7 calendar days
BAC / TWG
16Financial Bid Evaluation (LCRB or MEARB)▾
BAC evaluates financial components using either the Lowest Calculated Responsive Bid (LCRB) or Most Economically Advantageous Responsive Bid (MEARB) method.
LCRB Method (Sec. 61)
  • Bids ranked from lowest to highest calculated price
  • Lowest = Lowest Calculated Bid (LCB)
  • Must be ≤ ABC (price ceiling)
  • No lower limit on price
  • Used for straightforward goods/infra
MEARB Method (Sec. 61) — NEW
  • Quality-price ratio evaluation
  • Price weight: minimum 15%, max 40%
  • Quality criteria: technical merit, aesthetics, accessibility, social/environmental, delivery conditions, disposal
  • Best overall score = MEAB
  • Used for complex/high-value/quality-sensitive procurements
Sec. 60 — ABC as ceiling Sec. 61 — LCRB / MEARB evaluation Sec. 62 — Consulting (HRB) Abstract of Bids; BAC Resolution
BAC / TWG
17Post-Qualification▾
BAC verifies and validates that the bidder with the LCB/MEAB/HRB meets all requirements in the bidding documents. Must be completed within 12 calendar days.
Post-Qual Verification (Sec. 63)
  • Verify legal eligibility documents
  • Verify technical eligibility (experience, equipment)
  • Verify financial capacity
  • Site visit / inspection (if needed)
  • Cross-check with government databases (BIR, DTI, CIAP, SEC via Sec. 23)
  • Verify beneficial ownership declarations
Outcomes
  • Pass: Bid declared LCRB / MEARB / HRRB
  • Fail: Post-disqualified; proceed to next-ranked bidder
  • Repeat until LCRB/MEARB/HRRB determined or all bidders disqualified (→ Failed Bidding)
Sec. 63 — Post-Qualification (≤12 calendar days) Sec. 64 — Failure of Bidding Must complete within 12 calendar days
⬡ Decision Gate
Is a responsive bidder found after post-qualification?
✓ YES — Proceed to Award ✗ Failure of Bidding — Re-bid (2nd bidding) After 2nd failure → Negotiated Procurement (Sec. 35)

Phase 6 — Award & Contract Execution

BAC recommends award to HoPE; Notice of Award issued; winning bidder posts performance security; contract is signed; Notice to Proceed is issued.

RA 12009 — Article XI | Secs. 66–71
BAC → HoPE
18BAC Recommends Award to HoPE▾
BAC prepares a resolution recommending contract award to the LCRB/MEARB/HRRB bidder and transmits to HoPE (or duly authorized representative) for approval.
BAC Resolution Must Include
  • Name of winning bidder
  • Award basis (LCRB, MEARB, or HRRB)
  • Contract amount (≤ ABC)
  • Summary of evaluation results
  • Observers' reports attached
HoPE Action (Sec. 66)
  • Approve or disapprove within 10 calendar days
  • Disapproval must be in writing with valid, reasonable, and justifiable grounds (copy to BAC)
  • GOCCs: Board action within 20 days
Sec. 66 — Notice & Execution of Award Sec. 42(h) — BAC Recommendation BAC Resolution; Notice of Award HoPE action: ≤10 calendar days
Winning Bidder / Supplier
19Notice of Award (NOA) Issued & Bidder Accepts▾
HoPE issues the Notice of Award to the winning bidder. Bidder formally accepts within the period stated in the bidding documents.
After NOA Issuance
  • Winning bidder notified immediately
  • Other bidders notified (bid securities returned)
  • NOA posted on PhilGEPS and agency website
  • Bidder must accept and post performance security within 10 calendar days
Period of Action on Procurement (Sec. 67)
  • Bid opening → contract award: ≤60 calendar days
  • GPPB may extend up to 90 calendar days
  • BAC members on "jury duty" until NOA issued
Sec. 66 — NOA issuance Sec. 67 — 60-day period of action Notice of Award (NOA)
Winning Bidder
20Post Performance Security▾
Winning bidder posts the required Performance Security before contract signing. Form and amount specified in the bidding documents / GPPB guidelines.
Acceptable Forms (Sec. 68)
  • Cash or cashier's check
  • Manager's check
  • Bank draft / guarantee
  • Irrevocable standby letter of credit
  • Surety bond from IC-identified surety company (callable-on-demand)
  • GSIS bond
  • For special sectors (Sec. 76): performance securing declaration
Key Requirements
  • Posted within 10 calendar days of NOA
  • Must remain effective until final acceptance
  • IC-verified surety companies only
  • Callable-on-demand surety bonds (new requirement)
Sec. 68 — Performance Security Posted within 10 calendar days of NOA
Procurement Office + Supplier
21Contract Signing & Notice to Proceed (NTP)▾
Winning bidder formally enters into contract within 10 calendar days of NOA. Notice to Proceed issued within 3 calendar days of contract approval.
Contract Signing (Sec. 66)
  • Within 10 calendar days of NOA receipt
  • Higher authority approval: 15 calendar days (NGAs) / 20 days (GOCCs)
  • Disapproval must be in writing
  • Contract includes liquidated damages clause (Sec. 97)
  • Fixed-price contract; price escalation only with GPPB approval
Notice to Proceed (NTP)
  • Issued within 3 calendar days of contract approval
  • Effective only upon receipt by contractor
  • Marks start of contract implementation period
  • Warranty period begins from this point
Sec. 66 — Contract Execution Sec. 89 — Fixed Contract Prices Sec. 97 — Liquidated Damages clause Contract Agreement; NTP
🚚

Phase 7 — Contract Implementation & Delivery

Supplier/contractor delivers goods or performs services according to contract terms. Procuring entity monitors performance and inspects deliverables.

RA 12009 — Articles XI, XVIII | Secs. 71, 89–90
Supplier / Contractor
22Delivery / Performance of Contract▾
Supplier delivers goods or performs services per the agreed schedule in the contract. All deliveries must be brand new unless specifically excepted.
Supplier Obligations
  • Deliver to specified locations on schedule
  • Goods must be brand new (Sec. 7)
  • Comply with technical specifications
  • Maintain quality standards throughout
  • For subcontracting: prior HoPE approval (Sec. 7)
  • Subcontractors must meet eligibility criteria
Monitoring
  • End-user/PMO monitors delivery schedule
  • Record deliveries in system
  • Flag delays (liquidated damages may apply)
  • For Infrastructure: CPES applied to subcontractors (Sec. 7)
  • Procurement monitoring report (BAC, semiannual)
Sec. 71 — Contract Implementation (per IRR) Sec. 89 — Fixed prices Sec. 97 — Liquidated Damages System: Delivery Tracking Module
Supplies / Property Office + End-User
23Inspection, Testing & Acceptance▾
Inspection and Acceptance Committee (IAC) or designated officer inspects and tests the delivered goods or completed work against technical specifications and contract terms.
Inspection Process
  • Inspect quantity vs. delivery receipt
  • Test quality vs. specifications
  • Check for defects or damage
  • Verify brand new condition (Sec. 7)
  • Verify completeness of accessories, manuals
  • Infrastructure: joint inspection with contractor
Acceptance Documents
  • Inspection and Acceptance Report (IAR)
  • Certificate of Acceptance
  • Property Acknowledgment Receipt (PAR) / ICS
  • Report of Survey (if partial rejection)
  • Deficiency notice (if items rejected)
Sec. 90 — Warranty IAR; Certificate of Acceptance System: Inventory & Warehouse Module (Sec. 19)
📦
Cloud-Based Inventory (Sec. 19): Accepted items automatically updated in the cloud-based inventory system coordinated with DICT. Property accountability records maintained digitally.
⬡ Decision Gate
Are all delivered items accepted (fully compliant with specs)?
✓ Fully Accepted — Proceed to Payment ✗ Partial/Rejection — Return to Supplier for Replacement / Apply Liquidated Damages
💳

Phase 8 — Acceptance & Payment Processing

Final acceptance, payment document preparation, obligation, electronic payment to supplier, and warranty monitoring.

RA 12009 — Secs. 21, 90 | COA & DBM Rules
End-User + Supplies / Property Office
24Final Acceptance & Issuance of IAR▾
End-user unit and property office formally accept the goods/services and issue the Inspection and Acceptance Report, which triggers the payment process.
Acceptance Documents
  • Signed Inspection & Acceptance Report (IAR)
  • Certificate of Completion (infrastructure)
  • Final Acceptance Certificate
  • Property Acknowledgment Receipt or ICS
  • Updated inventory records
Warranty Activation (Sec. 90)
  • Warranty period starts from final acceptance
  • Retention money held until warranty expires
  • For infrastructure: contractor assumes full responsibility
  • Defects must be repaired within 90 calendar days
Sec. 90 — Warranty (Goods & Infrastructure) IAR; Certificate of Acceptance
End-User / Implementing Unit
25Prepare Payment Documents▾
End-user unit prepares and forwards all supporting documents for payment processing to the Accounting Office.
Payment Document Package
  • Disbursement Voucher (DV)
  • Contract / Purchase Order copy
  • Sales Invoice / Official Receipt
  • Delivery Receipts
  • Inspection & Acceptance Report (IAR)
  • Certificate of Acceptance
  • Obligation Request & Status (ORS)
  • PhilGEPS Reference Number
  • Performance Security reference
Compliance Checks
  • Documents routed to Accounting via system
  • COA auditing requirements compliance
  • Tax withholding computation
  • Supplier's BIR/TIN verification
DV; IAR; OR/SI; ORS System: Document Management & Routing
Accounting Office
26Accounting Review, Obligation & Processing▾
Accounting Office reviews documents for completeness, correctness, and compliance; records the obligation; prepares for payment.
Accounting Actions
  • Review payment documents for completeness
  • Verify obligation vs. available budget
  • Record journal entries
  • Compute withholding taxes (EWT, VAT)
  • Certify availability of funds
  • Sign Disbursement Voucher
COA Pre-Audit (if required)
  • COA resident auditor review (for applicable amounts)
  • COA audit trail maintained in PhilGEPS (Sec. 22)
  • COA can verify system security at any time
Sec. 22 — PhilGEPS audit trail for COA DV with complete certifications IFMIS / eNGAS integration
Accounting / Finance Office
27Electronic Payment to Supplier▾
Finance Office processes the electronic payment to the supplier through the government's Integrated Financial Management Information System (IFMIS).
Electronic Payment (Sec. 21)
  • Uniform electronic payment system (IFMIS)
  • Developed by DBM, COA, and DOF jointly
  • Net of withholding taxes
  • Retention money withheld (warranty period)
  • Payment confirmed and recorded
  • PhilGEPS updated with payment completion
Post-Payment
  • Official Receipt / Acknowledgment from supplier
  • Update books of accounts
  • Record in Registry of Disbursements
  • BIR Form 2307 (Certificate of Creditable Tax Withheld)
Sec. 21 — Electronic Payment System (IFMIS) IFMIS / LandBank / DBM ePayment Payment within 15 days of acceptance (COA rules)
💳
IFMIS Integration (Sec. 21): The digital procurement system must interface seamlessly with DBM's IFMIS for payment processing. PhilGEPS updated automatically upon payment completion for full transparency.
All Departments
28Warranty Monitoring, Retention Release & Case Closure▾
System monitors the warranty period, tracks defect reports, and releases retention money/performance security upon expiry of the warranty and full compliance.
Warranty Monitoring (Sec. 90)
  • System auto-tracks warranty expiry dates
  • End-user reports defects through system
  • Contractor notified automatically
  • Repair deadline tracked (90-day clock)
  • Government undertakes repair if contractor defaults
Case Closure
  • Release retention money after warranty period
  • Release performance security upon final acceptance
  • Update procurement monitoring report (BAC, semiannual)
  • PhilGEPS record of contract completion
  • Update CPES for contractor (infrastructure)
  • Archive documents (retain for COA audit)
Sec. 90 — Warranty Period & Retention Sec. 42(j) — BAC Procurement Monitoring Report System: Warranty Tracker; CPES integration (Sec. 23)
